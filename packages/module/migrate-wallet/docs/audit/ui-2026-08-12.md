### 1. SR-6 answers a pixel hazard by destroying state: a phone call silently voids the user's handwritten backup

**Impact** 3/4 · **Size** 2/4 (Fix A only — see "Sizing") · **Status** new — no register row, no spec id
**Evidence** `components/MigrateWalletWizard.tsx:477-494` (the effect), `:270,:296` (regeneration),
`:433-438` (the cancel invariant) · `node_modules/react-native-web/src/exports/AppState/index.js`
· `packages/module/app-activity-mobile/src/store/{reload-gate.ios.ts:21,side-effects.ts:22}`
· `packages/module/vault-in-memory-ui/src/pages/RecoveryPhrase/RecoveryPhrase.tsx:19-67`

The SR-6 teardown arms only on `backupPhrase`/`verifyPhrase`, guards with
`if (nextState === 'active') return`, then clears `pendingPassword`, clears `pendingPhrase`, and
calls `cancelWizard()`.

**What the auditor found** — _"That code says: 'if the app stops being the thing on screen, throw
away the user's recovery phrase and close the whole flow.' The problem is that 'stops being the
thing on screen' is a much bigger set of events than the author had in mind — and the price the
user pays is bigger than the author thought."_

**On iOS.** `inactive` does not mean the user left; it means something briefly appeared over the
app — an incoming call, a notification banner, the app switcher, Control Centre, a system alert.
The guard bails only on `'active'`, so every one of those falls through and runs the teardown.

_"Now picture the actual user. They're on the screen that shows 24 recovery words and says 'write
these down.' That is a slow, off-device task — the whole design invites the user to look away,
fumble for a pen, glance at their phone. Word 20 of 24, their phone rings. They decline the call.
They come back to… nothing. Flow closed, no explanation._

_And here's the part that turns an annoyance into a real problem: the phrase is generated fresh
each time, at `MigrateWalletWizard.tsx:270` and `:296`. Restarting doesn't give back the same
words — it gives different words. So the user is now holding a piece of paper with 20 words that
secure nothing, and they have no idea it's dead."_

> **The auditor revised this on review, before scoring.** Their first pass concluded "if they ever
> trust that paper, they lose their funds." They withdrew it and asked for the correction to land
> before the severity did: _"Not funds loss. Two things block it: nothing exists yet — the teardown
> only fires on `backupPhrase`/`verifyPhrase`, before the destination wallet is created, so the
> phrase that dies belongs to no wallet and no money was ever in it. And `verifyPhrase` catches bad
> paper — if the user restarts and writes down a half-old/half-new phrase, the verify step checks it
> against the app's current phrase and fails. They can't proceed on a broken backup."_ Both blockers
> were verified (verification 9). The retraction is kept on the record because the second-order path
> below is what carries the severity, and it is easy to mistake for the first.

**On the extension.** Web has no `inactive` — only `background`, when the tab genuinely goes
hidden. Narrower, and the trigger is honest. But _"the most common reason to switch tabs on a
'write these down' screen is to open your password manager and paste them in. That's the careful
user, and we punish them. Meanwhile the user who photographs the screen with a second phone —
worse practice — sails through."_

**The remedy does not follow from the hazard.** _"The snapshot captures pixels. SR-6 answers it by
destroying state. Those aren't the same problem, and conflating them is where the user harm comes
from."_ Splitting signal from action resolves it, and makes `!== 'active'` **partly right**:

- `inactive` → **cover the screen.** This is exactly what `inactive` exists for on iOS: it fires
  _before_ the snapshot is taken. Drop an opaque overlay, keep every byte of state, cancel nothing.
  The user declines the call, comes back, the cover lifts, they are still on word 20.
- `background` → cover stays, and now the wipe question is live.

So SR-6 detects the right moment and wires the wrong action to it. The `'background'` gate applies
to the **destructive** action; the cover may keep the loose check — that is the one place
`!== 'active'` is correct.

**On background, clear — with two non-optional conditions.** With no app lock in these hosts,
returning from background hands the words to whoever holds the device, so leaving the phrase
rendered is not defensible on native. _"Two conditions attached, and neither is optional: it must
not fire on `inactive`, and it cannot be silent. Because of `:270`/`:296`, a wipe means the paper
in the user's hand is dead and only the app knows it. No amount of covering fixes that one."_

**The reframe — SR-6 is the wrong size at exactly one screen.** _"I checked the other place these
words appear. `vault-in-memory-ui/…/RecoveryPhrase.tsx` — settings, 'show my recovery phrase,'
same 24 words, for a wallet that already holds funds — has no AppState handling at all. Neither
does `RecoveryPhraseVerification`. No FLAG_SECURE, no expo-screen-capture, nothing, anywhere in
either host._

_So the app's actual posture on this hazard is unmitigated, and SR-6 is a lone exception guarding
the cheapest instance of it — a wallet that doesn't exist yet and holds nothing — while the
expensive instance is wide open. Its only protection is a manual `handleToggleBlur`._

_That makes SR-6 wrong either way. If the snapshot hazard justifies destroying user progress here,
it justifies protection on the funded-wallet screen too, and this is under-scoped. If it doesn't,
it can't justify the cost here, and this is over-priced. It can't be the right size at exactly one
screen."_

**Where the fix belongs — not in the wizard.**

| Platform | Remedy                                                                                                                         |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Android  | `FLAG_SECURE` on the phrase surfaces — blanks the recents thumbnail and blocks screenshots. Small, total, zero user cost.      |
| iOS      | No OS equivalent; cover-on-`inactive` is the only real answer, so it is a shared component applied to **every** phrase screen. |
| Web      | Nothing needed — no snapshot exists.                                                                                           |

SR-6 then shrinks to what only it can do: clear `pendingPassword` on `'background'`, and tell the
user the words are void.

**Why it matters — the auditor's filing: High UX / Low direct financial.** _"It's a completion-rate
and trust defect, not a 'user loses money' bug. The security concern is second-order: the workaround
it provokes is more dangerous than the risk it prevents."_ Three reasons it is worse than annoying:

- _"The user didn't do anything wrong. An incoming call kills the flow. Unavoidable, repeatable, no
  explanation shown."_
- _"It hits mid-migration, when funds are being moved off an old wallet. People who bail here are
  left half-moved."_
- _"It pushes users toward screenshots. Get burned twice, and the natural fix is to screenshot the
  words so you can't lose them. Screenshots sync to iCloud/Google Photos. That is a real funds-loss
  path — and this control causes it."_

One line for a standup: _"we treat a phone call as a security breach, and the punishment is silently
voiding the user's handwritten backup."_

**Sizing — two fixes, very different sizes.** The 2/4 above scores **Fix A only**, because the
auditor scoped Fix B out of this module: _"don't let it ride on the migrate-wallet ticket — it isn't
that module's bug to fix."_

**Fix A — stop the bleeding.** Change the gate to `'background'`, keep clearing the password, tell
the user the words are void. One file plus a new string through i18n. **~1 day, ships
independently — do not block it behind anything below.**

**Fix B — the real fix.** A shared "hide sensitive screen" primitive, applied everywhere a recovery
phrase is shown: iOS cover-on-`inactive` (no OS flag exists), Android `FLAG_SECURE` (new dependency
plus an expo config plugin), extension nothing. Six candidate screens across three modules —
migrate-wallet (`BackupPhraseStep`, `VerifyPhraseStep`, `SourceSeedStep`), vault-in-memory-ui
(`RecoveryPhrase`, `RecoveryPhraseVerification`), account-management
(`RestoreWalletRecoveryPhraseSheet`). The auditor flagged that list as a keyword sweep: _"someone
should confirm each one actually renders words before committing to the number."_ It has not been
confirmed — see verification 10.

Rough sizing: spike (does the iOS cover beat the snapshot?) ~1 day, **first, because it decides
everything downstream**; shared component across 3 platforms 2-4 days if the spike is clean; rollout
plus Storybook tests ~2 days; manual device QA ~1 day. **~1.5-2 weeks, one engineer.** What could
double it:

- **The iOS race.** If a React render does not land before iOS grabs the snapshot, the overlay has to
  be native, installed in `applicationWillResignActive` — pushing this to 3-4 weeks. This is the whole
  reason to spike first.
- **`FLAG_SECURE` is a product decision, not just a technical one.** It is activity-wide unless
  toggled, and it blocks the user screenshotting their own phrase. Someone has to say yes to that.
- **It cannot be proven in CI.** Verifying an app-switcher snapshot needs a human holding two real
  devices. Budget the QA time explicitly.

The auditor's framing for a manager: ship Fix A this sprint; scope Fix B as "protect recovery phrases
app-wide", size it after the spike, and note that the hazard it addresses is _"already accepted,
unmitigated, on the funded-wallet phrase screen in settings."_

**Verification** — Verified at `f881de3eb`. This finding was revised twice under verification; both
revisions are recorded because they change its scope.

1. **The guard and the teardown are as described.** `:486` is `if (nextState === 'active') return;`
   followed by `setPendingPassword(undefined)`, `setPendingPhrase(undefined)`, `cancelWizard()`,
   armed only on `backupPhrase`/`verifyPhrase` (`:483`).
2. **Regeneration confirmed.** `:270` and `:296` both call `util.generateMnemonicWords()`, so a
   restart yields different words. The dead-paper consequence follows directly.
3. **The cancel invariant is exact**, and the auditor's fairness point about it stands: `:433-438`
   is `hasLostPhrase = (step === 'backupPhrase' || step === 'verifyPhrase') && pendingPhrase ===
undefined → cancelWizard()`. Clearing the phrase on those steps already implies cancelling, so
   the explicit call is redundant-but-visible rather than the defect. **The fix is not "stop
   cancelling."**
4. **Web AppState confirmed.** `react-native-web`'s `AppState` derives from
   `document.visibilityState` — `hidden`/`prerender`/`unloaded` → `'background'`, else `'active'`.
   There is no `'inactive'` on web, so the platform split is real.
5. **First revision — argument rebuilt on stronger ground.** The auditor originally grounded the
   inconsistency in `packages/next/ui/src/hooks/useAppLock.ts:71` (gate on `appState ===
'background'`; comment "Transient `inactive` (biometric prompt, control center): pause, don't
   lock.") and `apps/lace-next/src/Main.tsx` (`<LockGate privacyCover={<Splash />}>`). Both quotes
   are **exact**, but both live in `apps/lace-next`, a **separate application** that does not depend
   on `@lace-module/migrate-wallet`; neither `apps/lace-mobile` nor `apps/lace-extension` depends on
   `@lace-next/ui` or contains `LockGate`/`useAppLock`. The argument survives on **local** evidence
   instead, which is stronger: every `AppState` gate in the wizard's own host family acts on a
   specific state — `reload-gate.ios.ts:21` and `app-activity-mobile/side-effects.ts:22` on
   `'background'`; `side-effects.ts:31`, `init.ts:25`, `defer-biometric-prompt…:15` and
   `useReportOnlineStatus.ts:44` on `'active'`; `reload-gate.android.ts:23` on the transition into
   `'active'`. `MigrateWalletWizard.tsx:486` is the **only** consumer in the app that acts on
   "anything that isn't active", and the only one whose action is destructive.
6. **Second revision — the "already covered twice" argument inverted, and the auditor withdrew it
   for native.** There is no privacy cover, no `preventScreenCapture`/`FLAG_SECURE`, and no
   lock-on-background anywhere in `lace-mobile`, `lace-extension`, or the packages they consume
   (searched; the only hits were e2e page objects). So the snapshot hazard is **not** mitigated a
   layer up in these hosts, and "leave the phrase alone on background" would be a real regression
   on native. The auditor accepted this and split the recommendation by platform.
7. **The reframe is confirmed, and it is the strongest part.** `packages/module/vault-in-memory-ui`
   and `packages/module/account-management` contain **zero** `AppState` references between them —
   that is the whole settings-side phrase surface, including
   `RecoveryPhrase.tsx` (whose only protection is `isBlurred`/`handleToggleBlur`/`onToggleBlur` at
   `:19-67`, a manual toggle), `RecoveryPhraseVerification.tsx` (zero), and
   `RestoreWalletRecoveryPhraseSheet.tsx`. `MigrateWalletWizard.tsx` is therefore the sole
   `AppState`-driven phrase protection in the host family, on the one instance where the wallet does
   not yet exist.
8. **The proposed primitive is reachable.** `SheetSafeOverlay` ships in
   `@lace-lib/ui-toolkit` (a migrate-wallet dependency, `package.json:27`), is itself `AppState`-aware,
   and is already consumed by two sibling modules in this host family —
   `authentication-prompt-ui-v2-extension/src/components/PasswordAuthPromptContainer.tsx` and
   `air-gapped-qr-exchange-host/src/AirGappedQrExchangeHostOverlay.tsx`. The component exists in
   these hosts; only the gate does not.
9. **Third revision — both fund-loss blockers confirmed, so the severity is UX, not financial.**
   (a) The teardown arms only on `backupPhrase`/`verifyPhrase` (`:483`), both of which precede
   `creatingDestination`, so the phrase destroyed belongs to no wallet. (b) `VerifyPhraseStep.tsx:47-48`
   is `enteredWords.length === words.length && enteredWords.every((word, index) => word === words[index])`,
   compared against the `words` prop — which is the app's **current** `pendingPhrase`. A regenerated
   phrase therefore fails verification and the user cannot proceed on dead paper. The auditor's
   retraction is correct on both counts.
10. **The six-screen list is not confirmed, and my classification was inconclusive** — the auditor's
    caveat stands as work, not as a resolved question. A crude display-vs-input heuristic separated
    `BackupPhraseStep` (displays words) from `VerifyPhraseStep` and `SourceSeedStep` (accept typed
    words), but returned nothing for `RecoveryPhrase.tsx` and `RestoreWalletRecoveryPhraseSheet.tsx`,
    both of which delegate rendering to shared components. Fix B's scope still needs a screen-by-screen
    pass.
11. **The teardown is mis-scoped inside this module too, which sharpens the reframe.** `:483` arms it
    on `backupPhrase`/`verifyPhrase` and **not** on `enterSeed` — the screen where the user types the
    **source** wallet's recovery phrase. That is the phrase controlling the wallet that actually holds
    the funds being migrated, and it is the module's only phrase surface with no `AppState` handling at
    all. So within migrate-wallet the control guards the phrase worth nothing and leaves the phrase
    worth everything unguarded — the same inversion the auditor identified across modules, reproduced
    at a smaller scale. Any Fix B scope must include `SourceSeedStep`.

**What the two users share**

- The indicators **never agree on the first screen**. "Step 1 of 4" reads 25%; the rail beside

  path is the shortest possible one.
- **The rail stalls precisely where the counter moves.** Both users see the same rail width on

  bar is most legible as "nothing happened".

- **They imply different journey lengths.** B's rail has 3 stops; B's counter promises 4.

### 4. Paste wipes the clipboard silently, then a second Paste is a silent no-op

**Impact** 3/4 · **Size** 2/4 (Fix A only — see "Sizing") · **Status** new — no register row, no spec id
**Evidence** `components/PhraseField.tsx:96-119,164-166,178-185` ·
`components/SourceSeedStep.tsx:28-29,41,92-94` · `components/MigrateWalletWizard.tsx:687-697` ·
`packages/lib/ui-toolkit/src/design-system/util/index.ts:22-33` ·
`contract/i18n/…/en.json:83` · `test/components/SourceSeedStep.test.tsx:20-22`

The Paste control exists to wipe the clipboard after reading it — `SourceSeedStep.tsx:92-94`:
_"this phrase arrives via the clipboard, so routing it through a control that wipes afterwards beats
the OS shortcut."_ It is offered only on the source-seed field, never on verification.

**What the auditor found** — press two: nothing happens, and nothing is said.

The trace through `PhraseField.tsx:96-119`:

1. _`Clipboard.getStringAsync()` — the clipboard is empty because Lace emptied it on press one. Empty
   is not an error. `ExpoClipboard.getStringAsync()` resolves `''`; `navigator.clipboard.readText()`
   resolves `''`. Neither throws, so the `catch` never runs._
2. _`setHasPasteFailed(false)` — this fires **before** the empty check, so an empty read is actively
   classified as a success and any prior notice is cleared._
3. _`if (!clipboardText) return;` — `''` is falsy. Early return._
4. _`onChangeText` is never called. The field stays empty, the word count stays `''` (0 words renders
   blank, `PhraseField.tsx:164-166`), no error slot populates, the button doesn't change state._

_The user presses Paste and the screen does not move. It is behaviourally identical to a dead button._

_What they're told: nothing. The one string in the feature — "Lace couldn't read the clipboard. Paste
with your keyboard instead." — renders only under `hasPasteFailed` (`:178`), which is only set in the
`catch`, which this path cannot reach. **It is unreachable by construction here.**_

_And if it did fire, its advice is wrong._ ⌘V / Ctrl-V / long-press-Paste read the same empty
clipboard, and on mobile the OS paste affordance generally isn't offered for an empty pasteboard.
_"The only diagnostic copy in the feature prescribes a fallback that is guaranteed to fail in the exact
state the feature creates."_

The state is nondeterministic too: `try { await Clipboard.setStringAsync(''); } catch {}` swallows a
failed wipe. _"Writing an empty string is not uniformly supported across web clipboard
implementations, so on some platforms the wipe silently doesn't happen. From the user's side, press two
therefore either does nothing or refills the field — and there is no signal distinguishing the two.
Neither the user nor a developer reading a bug report can tell which occurred."_

Getting to press two is ordinary, not exotic:

- _The paste landed wrong._ Extra whitespace, a partial copy, the wrong password-manager entry. They
  clear the field to retry — and the source is gone.
- _The component resets itself._ `phrase` is local `useState('')` (`SourceSeedStep.tsx:41`), rendered

**What the auditor found** — _"They fail by two different bugs, and es-ES trips both."_

**Bug 1 — de-DE and 16 others: the two separators collapse into one character.**
`toLocaleString()` correctly groups the whole part with the locale's separator, then
`format-amounts.ts:36` joins the fraction with a **literal `.`**:

|                            | ICU truth   | `formatAda` renders |
| -------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------- |
| de-DE                      | `1.234,567` | **`1.234.567 ADA`** |
| token row directly beneath | `1.234,567` | `1.234,567` ✓       |

_"Group separator and decimal separator are now the same glyph. A German reader parses
`1.234.567 ADA` as one million two hundred thirty-four thousand — **1000× the real amount, on the
headline figure of a one-way irreversible sweep.** Four rows carry it; the token row directly beneath
renders the identical quantity correctly."_

**Bug 2 — a detection bug inside `getLocaleSeparators` itself.** Spanish CLDR sets
`minimumGroupingDigits: 2`, so `Intl.NumberFormat('es-ES').format(1234.5)` → `1234,5`, with **no
group separator at all**. The probe at `format-number.ts:30-39` assumes the test number yields both.
Stripping digits leaves `","` — one character — so `separators[0]` hands the **decimal** separator back
as `groupSeparator`, and `separators[1]` is `undefined`, falling through to `decimalSeparator = '.'`.
**es-ES is served en-US separators.**

| es-ES                              | ICU truth  | renders        |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------- | -------------- |
| four ADA rows (`formatAda`)        | `1234,567` | `1234.567 ADA` |
| token row (`formatAmountToLocale`) | `1234,567` | `1,234.567`    |

_"Neither is es-ES. Worse, `.` **is** the es-ES group separator, so the ADA rows misread by 1000×
exactly as in German — via a completely different code path."_

Three notes change the shape of the finding:

- _"**The separators come from the device, not from the app's language.**"_ Both `toLocaleString()` and
  `getLocaleSeparators()` are called with no locale argument, so both read the runtime default. _"This
  is not gated on Lace shipping a German or Spanish translation — English-language Lace on a German
  phone renders `1.234.567 ADA` today. The en/es/ja locale files are irrelevant to this half."_
- _"**Only comma-group/period-decimal locales render the two rows identically** — en-US, en-GB, es-MX,
  ja, ko, zh, hi, he, th. That is why it survived review, and why no story catches it: there is no test
  anywhere referencing `formatAda`, so nothing locks the current output or would flag a fix."_
- _"**One thing I could not verify.** `total / LOVELACE_PER_ADA` is a `BigInt`, so line 35 calls

  above) group as shown. There is no Intl polyfill anywhere… so whether Hermes routes BigInt's
  `toLocaleString` through Intl needs a device check. If it degrades to plain digits, mobile gets a
  third rendering: `1234.567 ADA`, ungrouped in every locale, under a grouped token row. **Either way
  the two halves of the card disagree.**"_

Bug 2 is not in this module, and is platform-wide enough to need its own ticket. _"Its reach is every caller of `formatAmountToLocale` / `valueToLocale` /

**Evidence** `store/helpers/count-script-utxos.ts:38-61` · `store/slice.ts:100-106` ·

**What the auditor found** — mechanically, the "3" is script-credential UTxO entries observed under a stake credential, summed

**What "3" can mean** — the auditor's enumeration:

1. **Real, self-created locked value** — a DeFi position, a timelock, a native-script multisig. _"This is one
   of eight readings, not the default one."_
2. **Someone else's dust.** _"No secret is required to construct an address combining an arbitrary script with
   the user's stake key and send min-UTxO to it."_ So the 3 can be entirely third-party — spam, or a
   deliberate lure. **The only direction the number can be wrong upward, under a stranger's control,
   unbounded.**
3. **A floor, not a total.** The fetch is stake-scoped, so a script UTxO at an enterprise address, or one whose

4. **"3, plus an unknown number we failed to fetch."** Two silent zero-collapse granularities: per reward
   account (`result.isErr() ? 0` — one key's failure contributes 0 while others still sum, so a
   multi-delegation account 0 under-counts) and whole-chain (`catchError(() => of(0))` — _"discards every
   successfully-fetched count in the `combineLatest`, including ones already in hand"_). Because the scanned
   half is added afterward, 3 can be "3 real from the scan + a total provider failure on account 0 recorded as
   zero." _"The rationale ('a disclosure must not fail an otherwise healthy migration') is sound; what does not
   follow from it is that the degraded result should be rendered identically to the healthy one."_
5. **Not a magnitude.** _"3 could be three min-UTxO dust entries, or one 200,000-ADA position that happens to
   sit in three outputs."_ No ADA, no assets, no range — _"the only bare integer on a screen that formats every
   other figure, and it's the one attached to loss."_
6. **Not a location.** No address, no account index, no link. A user who wants to check cannot.
7. **Not "everything that stays behind", despite the label.** Script UTxOs are one subset of `franken`
   (everything not an owned key hash). Also dropped and disclosed nowhere as a figure: UTxOs at non-owned
   key-hash credentials — which by the code's own admission includes **genuinely owned addresses past the
   payment gap of 100**. For account 0 it is worse: the store holds only the pre-filtered set, so account 0's
   non-script dropped UTxOs are unrecoverable for disclosure. _"The parenthetical '(these stay behind)' is
   true; the exhaustiveness a reader infers from it is not."_
8. **A discovery-time snapshot**, replayed by a pinned plan. Anything arriving between review and sweep is not
   in it.

_"Readings 1-8 render as the same three pixels."_

**What an absent row means.** `scriptUtxoCount > 0` is the entire render condition over
`discovery?.scriptUtxoCount ?? 0`, so absence is the union of: zero observed · observation failed at either
granularity · observation not attempted (`rewardAccounts.length === 0` short-circuits to `of(0)` with no
query) · not observable (scripts outside the stake-scoped view, or accounts past the 10-index gap frontier) ·
no discovery object.

_"So the absent row is not a negative finding. It is the absence of a positive one, and the design has already
taught the user otherwise: showing the row **only when non-zero** establishes 'row appears when there's
something to worry about', which makes its absence read as an all-clear it cannot support. Nothing on the
screen says 'we checked and found none' versus 'we could not check.'"_

And the scope statement that would qualify it appears on the wrong screen: `scannedThroughAccountIndex` renders
on `DoneSummary.tsx:123` — _"the user is told how far the scan reached **after** the irreversible action, not
before it."_

The shape of it: _"Every failure path in this number resolves to zero. The single path that inflates it is
a third party paying dust. **The number is therefore least trustworthy in exactly the direction that makes an
irreversible sweep look safer than it is, and most trustworthy when someone hostile wants it to look
worse.**"_

The design already has the slot for the caveat: `FactRow` takes a `note`, and the sibling stake-deposit row
uses it for an explainer (`ReviewSummary.tsx:293-295`). The script row — _"the one whose type carries two
explicit disclaimers"_ — passes `label` and `value` only. _"The slot for the caveat exists, is used for the
lower-stakes row, and is empty on the higher-stakes one."_

**Why it matters** — 4/4, the only one in this report. _"The defect is not that the number is hard to read; it
is that the screen renders **'we could not check' and 'there is nothing' as the same pixels**, and the collapse
only ever runs toward the reassuring one. `catchError(() => of(0))` plus a `scriptUtxoCount > 0` render gate
means a provider blip on the source account's fetch produces an absent row, and an absent row is the same
absent row a genuinely clean wallet gets."_

The silence is a local choice, not a platform constraint, and the proof is one file over. _"The scan half
hits the same provider for the same class of data and deliberately does the opposite… The same provider failing

**What RNW reimplements of `<button disabled>` — and the one thing it doesn't.**

| guarantee        | reimplemented? | where                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| press inert      | ✅             | `PressResponder` gates `onStartShouldSetResponder` / `onKeyDown`                                                                                                                         |
| out of tab order | ✅             | `_tabIndex = disabled ? -1 : 0` (`:197`)                                                                                                                                                 |

_"So of the three interaction states, press is guarded by the platform, hover is guarded **twice** (the hook's
`if (!disabled)` plus RNW's own `useHover`), and focus is guarded **zero** times. The single guarantee RNW declines
to reimplement is precisely the one `use-interaction-state.ts` also leaves open. **The gaps compose; they don't
cancel.**"_

**The asymmetry has a documented origin, and the document is false.** The hook's docstring claims _"Hover **and
focus** state… The `Button` atom tracks **this** internally."_ It does not: the atom has `isHovered` only
(`button.tsx:36`), guarded by `if (!disabled && !loading)` (`:100`) — _"the exact line the hook copied down to its
guard."_ The atom has **no focus state at all**; the only focus token in the folder is `'focused'` in
`button.types.ts:14`, which nothing consumes.

_"The hover half was copied from a precedent that carried a guard. The focus half was **invented** with no precedent,
and arrived unguarded. The docstring's false claim that the atom tracks focus is what would stop a reviewer from
noticing an invention had happened."_

**What the user gets.** A disabled control rendering the focus affordance — and in both components that affordance is
deliberately the loudest signal available: thickened to 2px specifically so it _"reads at a glance for a keyboard
user"_ (`GlassNavButton.tsx:65-67`, `WizardOptionCard.tsx:111-113`), plus a flip to `theme.border.focused`. Nothing
clears it — no guard on entry, no reset when `disabled` flips.

**Live, not hypothetical:** `WizardFrame.tsx:160` `disabled={navigationDisabled}` ← `VerifyPhraseStep.tsx:80`

irreversible dispatch is in flight"_ (`WizardFrame.tsx:55-57`).

_"User submits the phrase, both exits freeze, it's taking a while, they click Cancel. The click lands on the child
(`box-none` keeps children hit-testable), the press is correctly swallowed — and the only thing that changes on

to a refused interaction on a deliberately frozen wizard.\*\* A user reads that as _registered, working on it_ — at the
exact moment the honest answer is _ignored, and by design, because this dispatch is irreversible._"\_

**Why the 71 `aria-disabled` sites are blind by construction, not by oversight.** _"`aria-disabled` is RNW's
unconditional mirror of the prop. `toHaveAttribute('aria-disabled','true')` is `expect(props.disabled).toBe(true)` in
a DOM costume: it can only fail if the wrong prop was passed. It cannot reach focus state, tab order, pointer-events,
or the ring."_

And the negative form is weaker still: `createDOMProps` emits the attribute only `if (disabled === true)`, so
_"enabled means **absent**, not 'false'. Every `.not.toHaveAttribute('aria-disabled','true')` — the 58 inline ones,
`expectEnabled`, and `waitForNotDisabled` — is **vacuously satisfied** by any element that never had the attribute: a
wrapper div, a Text node, a mis-resolved testID, a button that failed to render. **Enabled is the default verdict for
nothing at all.**"_ Which is why the e2e helper had to pair `waitForClickable()` with the attribute check —
_"one covers existence, one covers the claim, **neither covers behaviour.** Not one of the 71 clicks a disabled
control and asserts a no-op, or asserts focus doesn't land on it."_ `add-contact`'s comments narrate the attribute as
behaviour (`// Save button should be disabled`).

**The meta-finding — an escalation failure.** _"One surface diagnosed it exactly right — 'it's a `<div>`, not a
native `<button>`' — and filed the diagnosis as a **test-helper docstring**. A component-contract fact recorded in
the test layer's comments cannot reach the components, which is why 58 more copies of the workaround were written
without anyone re-deriving it. **The knowledge was in the repo the entire time.**"_

**One more thing, same shape.** Both components pass `accessibilityState={{ disabled }}` (`GlassNavButton.tsx:49`,
`WizardOptionCard.tsx:93`). _"It is inert on both platforms:"_ RNW 0.21 does not forward it (filtered by `View`'s
`pickProps` — silently dropped, no attribute, no warning), and native RN's `Pressable` overwrites it from `disabled`
anyway. _"So the components carry the prop that **looks like** the accessibility contract and does nothing, and lack
the guard that **looks like** a style detail and is the actual defect. **That ordering is the finding in
miniature.**"_

**The fix — one line, and a guard on `onFocus` alone is insufficient** because `isFocused` is already `true` when
`disabled` flips. Derive at read time and both paths close:

```ts
return {
  isHovered: isHovered && !disabled,
  isFocused: isFocused && !disabled,
  handlers,
};
```

_"Then the `onHoverIn` guard and the `[disabled]` memo dep both go away, and the hook becomes symmetric and **correct**
rather than symmetric and wrong."_ Plus correcting the false docstring and deleting the two inert
`accessibilityState` props.

**The check that sets the floor — not run, and it is not narrowing, it is load-bearing.** That a `mousedown` on a
child of a `tabindex="-1"`, `pointer-events:none` box focuses that box in Chrome/Safari is **inference from the
source, not observation.** `tabindex="-1"` is click-focusable by definition and hit-testing is a separate concern from
focusability, so the walk very likely reaches the box — but "very likely" is not verified. What settles it: _"Thirty
seconds in the Verify step with `isSubmitting` pinned true."_ This session could not run it (no browser).

### A1. `WizardOptionCard` has no path for `description` to reach the accessible name

**Impact** n/a — out of scope · **Size** n/a · **Status** verified mechanism, not a finding
· **Refs** none — no register row, no spec id
**Evidence** `components/WizardOptionCard.tsx:92,120` · `ChooseDestinationStep.tsx:41-49`
· `ConnectDeviceStep.tsx:275-287` · `ExistingWalletPicker.tsx:46-51`
· `node_modules/react-native/Libraries/Components/Pressable/Pressable.js:252`
· `node_modules/react-native-web/src/modules/createDOMProps/index.js:442-449`

**What the auditor found** — `Pressable.js:252` sets `accessible: accessible !== false`, so
this card is always a single accessibility element. Its children collapse into one node, and
when `accessibilityLabel` is present it replaces the text the children would otherwise
contribute. On the web build the same prop becomes `aria-label`, which wins the
accessible-name computation over the button's contents.

`accessibilityLabel={accessibilityLabel ?? title}` at `WizardOptionCard.tsx:92` is never
undefined for any caller. So the `<Text.S>` on line 120 is rendered, laid out, and never
spoken.

| Screen                 | On screen                                                                                      | Announced                     | Never spoken             |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Choose destination     | Create a new wallet / "Generate a brand new recovery phrase and wallet to receive your funds." | "Create a new wallet, button" | the recovery-phrase line |
| Connect device         | (Ledger wordmark) / "Nano S, Nano X"                                                           | "Ledger, button"              | the model list           |
| Existing wallet picker | My Wallet / "Ledger"                                                                           | "My Wallet, button"           | the wallet type          |

Trait ordering differs a little across VoiceOver / TalkBack / NVDA; the name is the same on
all three.

Each drop costs something different:

- **Destination** — the description is the only place the flow states that "Create a new
  wallet" generates a new recovery phrase. The two siblings lose "Sweep your funds into a
  wallet you already have in Lace" and "…into a Ledger, Trezor or other hardware wallet." A
  screen reader user picks between three options stripped down to their headlines.
- **Connect device** — this is the caller that tried hardest. `title` is deliberately
  undefined so the wordmark isn't read twice (`ConnectDeviceStep.tsx:279-281`), and
  `accessibilityLabel` exists precisely to restore the name. It restores the name and
  suppresses the model list — which for this row is the only text that answers "is my device
  one of these?" The prop's own doc comment ("Names the card for assistive tech when `title`
  is omitted") describes half of what it does.
- **Existing wallet picker** — the type label is what distinguishes two similarly-named
  wallets, and it's the thing that tells the user whether completing the sweep will require
  plugging in a device.

**The control case.** The fourth call site — blockchain selection at
`ConnectDeviceStep.tsx:215-224` — passes `title` with no `description`, and announces
correctly. That's the tell: nothing is wrong with the label plumbing. `description` is
simply not wired into the accessible name at all, and there is no prop combination a caller
can pass that would fix it — the only branch where children would be concatenated requires
both `accessibilityLabel` and `title` to be undefined, which no caller does and the prop
docs steer away from.

So it isn't three caller mistakes; it's one component contract that accepts a second line of
copy and has no path for it to reach assistive tech.

**Why it matters** — Every pick-one decision in the wizard runs through this component:
which destination wallet to create or reuse, which hardware device, which blockchain. A
screen-reader user makes each of those choices from the headline alone. On the existing-wallet
picker the suppressed line is the `Software`/`Ledger`/`Trezor` label, so two similarly-named
wallets are indistinguishable and the user cannot tell whether finishing the sweep will
require a physical device. On the destination screen the suppressed line is the only
statement that one option mints a new recovery phrase. This is the flow's entry decision,
ahead of an irreversible fund transfer.

**Verification** — Verified at `f881de3eb`.

1. `Pressable.js:252` reads `accessible: accessible !== false`, verbatim as claimed.
2. `createDOMProps/index.js:442-449` maps `accessibilityLabel` to the accessible name
   (`_ariaLabel = ariaLabel != null ? ariaLabel : accessibilityLabel`). Two refinements to
   the auditor's citation: the line is 442-449 rather than 421, and RNW additionally warns
   that `accessibilityLabel` is **deprecated in favour of `aria-label`** — so all four call
   sites also sit on a deprecated prop.
3. All four `WizardOptionCard` call sites enumerated repo-wide (`ChooseDestinationStep:41`,
   `ConnectDeviceStep:215` and `:275`, `ExistingWalletPicker:46`); none leaves both
   `accessibilityLabel` and `title` undefined, and three of the four pass a `description`.
   The claim is exhaustive, not sampled. The control case at `:215` passes no `description`
   and is correct.
4. Dropped copy confirmed verbatim in `packages/contract/i18n/src/translations/en.json:199,
201, 203`; `walletTypeLabel` resolves to `Software` | `Ledger` | `Trezor`
   (`wallet-type-label.ts`).
5. **Precedent, found during verification.** The shared toolkit already implements the
   correct pattern for this exact shape — `dropdownMenu.tsx:678-680` sets
   `` accessibilityLabel={titleSubText ? `${title}, ${titleSubText}` : title} `` on a
   `Pressable` with `accessibilityRole="button"`. This module does it too, in
   `ReviewSummary.tsx:70-72` (`` `${t('…wallet-name')}: ${name}` ``). So the fix is a
   one-line change matching an established in-repo pattern, not a novel design.
6. **No test would catch it.** No assertion on accessible names exists anywhere in this
   module — not in `test/` (whose `test/components/` holds four files, none for the option
   cards) nor in `apps/lace-mobile-storybook/stories/integration/migrate-wallet.stories.tsx`.

### Platform item — the i18n parity convention makes unreachable strings structurally mandatory

**The first correction in this audit that is not about migrate-wallet at all.** Split out of finding 15, and
recorded here because migrate-wallet is where it was found, not where it lives.

`packages/contract/i18n/src/translations/index.ts:114-120` enforces **bidirectional** structural parity through
`tsc`:

```ts
const _ja: typeof ja = en;
// `_ja` above only catches `en` missing a `ja` key; this catches the reverse —
// `ja` missing an `en` key (the gap that let 22 en/es keys ship without `ja`).
const _enJa: typeof en = ja;
```

The reverse check was added deliberately, to close a real gap. But **identity-based parity applied to
plural-suffixed keys is unsatisfiable for a language with fewer plural categories.** `Intl.PluralRules('ja')`
resolves one category, `other`; `select(1)` returns `other`. So a `_one` key in `ja.json` can never be selected —
and deleting it fails `_enJa`. _"ja is therefore **structurally required** to carry dead strings."_

**Five keys, four feature areas** — verified as the complete set of `_one` keys in `ja.json`:

```
migrate-wallet.phrase.word-count_one
migrate-wallet.phrase.unknown-words_one
dapp-connector.cardano.sign-tx.certificates-label_one
dapp-connector.cardano.sign-tx.proposal-procedures-label_one
v2.sheets.network-selection.blockchain-info_one
```

All five carry `ja-provenance.json` entries; the two migrate-wallet ones are tracked **machine-translated**. _"The
pipeline has produced, recorded and audited translations for strings that cannot render."_ And **`_other` appears
nowhere in any of the three locale files** (verified: zero occurrences), so the entire plural convention rides the
missing-key fallback rather than an explicit category.

Needs its own platform i18n ticket: either exempt plural suffixes from the identity check, or adopt explicit
`_other` keys so the categories are declared rather than inferred.

## A. Format deviations from the companion report, and why

This report and `domain-2026-08-11.md` share a directory and a template. A reader comparing them should
know where they diverge and on whose choice.

**Per-finding vocabulary — the companion is the correct model.** Measured across both:

| label                                       | domain report | this report, as recorded | after the collapse | of 18 findings              |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------ | --------------------------- |
| `Impact` · `Evidence` · `Verification`      | 2 / 2 / 2     | 19 / 19 / 18             | unchanged          | consistent in both          |
| **`What the auditor found`**                | **2 of 2**    | **9**                    | **13**             | the template's central slot |
| **`Why it matters`**                        | **2 of 2**    | **5**                    | **9**              | same                        |
| `Why 3/4` · `Why not 4/4` · `Why not 2/4` … | **0**         | **~40 one-offs**         | **19**             | entirely this report's      |

**One choice caused all of it.** The companion gives a number and moves on. This session instead offered
the auditor scoring _rungs_ and then preserved every rejection as a section. That single decision produced
three symptoms at once: the `Why not X/4` blocks **are** process-meta in the reading path; the auditor's
reasoning frequently landed inside them rather than in `What the auditor found`, so it is **restated in
both**; and the vocabulary stopped being uniform, so a reader cannot rely on finding the same thing in the
same place twice.

**Remediation is partial by decision, not unfinished by accident.** The collapse back to the template's five
labels — auditor's reasoning once, in `What the auditor found`; consequence in `Why it matters`; rejected
scoring rungs in Appendix B — was applied to the **six findings whose scoring debate had grown to four or
more sections**: 4, 6, 8, 9, 14, 16. Those six now share one label order,
`Impact · Evidence · What the auditor found · Why it matters · Sizing · Verification`, and in 14 and 16 it
also moved `Verification` back to the end of the finding, where every other finding has it.

The other twelve were left alone deliberately. Their scoring reasoning is a single paragraph that does
real work in place, and a full rewrite of the 2,500-line findings section risks dropping a verified
`file:line` — the one failure this document cannot absorb. So **19 one-off `Why not X/4` labels remain
inline**, down from ~40, and a reader will still meet them on findings 5, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 17 and 19.

**What the collapse did not do is shrink the file.** Moving a section to an appendix shortens the reading
path and leaves the line count where it was; the win is that a person fixing finding 8 no longer reads three
rejected rungs to reach the fix. Each collapsed finding keeps its full remediation scope inline — Fix A's
edit list in 4, the shared-key split in 16, the policy call in 8 — because that is what a fixer needs and it
is not scoring debate.

**`fix_order` lives in frontmatter, not as a prose section.** The dependency graph is genuinely missing
from both reports and no restructuring surfaces it — it had to be added. It went into frontmatter because
the companion has no such heading and the template defines none, so a new top-level section would be a
larger divergence than one more key alongside the keys this report already added

## B. Scoring rationale — policy

Each finding's **impact and size were chosen by the auditor**, and where a rung was rejected the reasoning
was recorded because it calibrates the scale for the next audit: the report's 2/4 band means _labels about
the flow_ (findings 2, 3, 10), 3/4 means _realized user harm today_, and 4/4 was reserved for
_unconditional or unrecoverable_ — reached once, by finding 8.

Three cross-cutting calibration rules emerged and are load-bearing when reading any score here:

1. **Breadth is priced under Size, never Impact.** (Finding 14 — nine strings across three locales did not
   raise its impact.)
2. **Sequencing is a dependency, never an impact.** (Findings 12, 13, 14 — "the refactor is already
   scheduled" was rejected as an impact argument three times.)
3. **A consequence already scored elsewhere is not re-charged.** (Finding 13 — the offered 4/4 rung
   double-counted finding 5's inflated figure.)
